Purpose:
  Calibrate thermistors & INA228 boards in a temperature controlled bath.

Equipment:
    Microcontroller (mcu):
      Wemos Lolin ESP32-S3

    INA228 power monitors, 4x:
      Adafruit INA228 (ADA5832).
      I2C 85V, 20-bit High or Low Side Power Monitor featuring Texas Instruments
      INA228.
      NOTE: Replaced the onboard shunt resistor of 0.015 Ohm with a 1.0 Ohm one.

    Thermistors, 4x:
      Measurement Specialties (GA)G22K7MCD419.
      Glass bead Ø0.38mm, 30 ms response time in liquids.

    Temperature bath:
      PolyScience PD15R-30-A12E.
      15 mK temperature stability.

    PT100 probe:
      Picotech PT-104.
      Accuracy: 0.015 K
      Resolution: 0.001 K

Each thermistor is connected to a single dedicated INA228 board in high-side
power configuration, meaning:
  V_bus & Vin+ are connected together to the 3.3V output of the mcu.
  Vin- is connected to one thermistor lead.
  Other thermistor lead is connected to the GND of the mcu.

We specifically keep each thermistor matched to a particular INA228 board. We
calibrate resistance to temperature by placing all thermistors inside the
temperature controlled bath, and calibrating their resistances against an
additional PT100 temperature probe (Picotech PT-104).

The bath temperature is set to a quasi-stationary ramps of 1 K / 10 min.

Findings after plotting results:
  Turns out that the down ramps have an offset with respect to the up ramps of
  about 0.2 K. This is probably due to the fact that the tip of the PT104 probe
  was inside a blind hole of a large copper body, intended to smoothen out
  temperature fluctuations inside of the temperature bath. The thermistors
  themselves were exposed directly to the water. This caused a thermal inertia
  of the PT104 probe with respect to the thermistors, causing the offset.

  Solution:
  Redo the calibration, but now with the tip of PT104 probe directly exposed to
  the bath water, with the thermistors right next to the probe tip.

Calibration 02-06-2026
----------------------
We use all new INA228 boards and all new thermistors. See date 11-05-2026 for
the used equipment. In addition, we now also log the INA228 die temperatures.

The thermistors are placed next to the PT104 probe inside of the temperature
bath and all are free to the water flow, i.e. not mounted inside of an
additional copper block, in order to minimize any thermal lag between PT104
probe and thermistor.

All INA228 boards are at ambient room temperature and they lie next to each
inside of a plastic box with two opposing vent holes with a fan blowing fresh
ambient air across. Hence, each INA228 board should experience identical die
temperatures with respect to each other, and any differences between the
reported die temperatures can then be attributed to the manufacturer's reported
accuracy of +/- 1 K.

In future calibration runs we will vary the ambient temperature of the INA228
dies and the onboard shunt resistors, and study its effect on the acquired
thermistor resistance values while keeping the thermistors at a fixed bath
temperature.

Analysis 03-06-2026
-------------------
After plotting results, turns out that the INA228 die temperatures were not
constant enough and actually fluctuated by 4 'C. The compressor of the
temperature bath heats up the surrounding ambient air too much and affects the
INA228 die temperatures. Must redo the calibration with a better temperature
stability of the INA boards.

Calibration 04-06-2026
----------------------
Redo calibration 02-06-2026, but this time with a better temperature stability
of the INA boards. The INA boards were placed inside a box with two opposing
openings of which one is hooked up to the air suction of the lab.

Analysis 05-06-2026
-------------------
After plotting results, turns out that the INA228 die temperatures were still
not constant enough and actually fluctuated by 3 'C. Again, the compressor
of the temperature bath generates so much heat that the overal ambient
temperature of the full lab increased as well.

Further analysis on the effect of T_die, see folder "260605_T_die_examination"
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
We have two sets of (R, T_PT104) data at a constant T_PT104 = 16 'C, but each at
a different constant T_die temperature. One set at around T_die ~ 25 'C, the
other at T_die ~ 28 'C. Using a Steinhart-Hart fit based on data set 2, we can
now compare the effect of T_die on the fitted temperatures.
  Data set 1: folder "260602_calibration"
  Data set 2: folder "260604_calibration"

Conclusion: After plotting results, turns out that T_die has no significant
effect on the fitted temperatures as long as T_die remains constant within 4 K,
and if we are content with a conservative absolute temperature accuracy of
0.1 K.
